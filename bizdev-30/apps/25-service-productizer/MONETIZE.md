# Service Productizer — How to Sell It

## Who buys it

Freelancers and 1–10 person service shops (web designers, marketers, bookkeepers, consultants, virtual assistants) who currently sell time — "$X/hour, scope TBD" — and are tired of watching a quoted project quietly grow into two. Their pain: every custom quote is a fresh negotiation, scope creep eats their margin every single month, and they know "productize your services" is good advice but have no structured way to actually do it. They already pay for Claude; they do not want another $30/month SaaS holding their pricing data.

## Positioning (pick one)

1. "Turn your next 'it depends' quote into a fixed-scope offer you can sell without a call."
2. "The scope-creep detector for people who price their own work — catches 'ongoing,' 'as needed,' and 'support' before a client does."
3. "Your Claude subscription just became a productization consultant: build the offer, tighten the language, write the sales page — no API key, nothing leaves your machine."

## Suggested pricing

- **One-time: $25–$45.** It pays for itself the moment one flagged "as needed" phrase saves a single unpaid revision round. Above $45, buyers start expecting invoicing/payment integration, which this deliberately does not do.
- Optional tiers: **Solo $29** (single user) · **Studio $69** (up to 5 seats, shared offer library across a small team).
- Bundle: pair with Pricing Bench and Proposal Studio as a "Price It Right" pack at $69–$89 — productize the offer here, package the price there, send the proposal.

## Where to sell free-to-start

- **Gumroad** — fastest start, discover feed, good for a pay-what-you-want launch week.
- **Lemon Squeezy** — cleaner checkout and EU VAT handled if selling into Europe.
- **Payhip** — no monthly fee, solid coupon tooling for a launch discount.
- **Ko-fi Shop** — good if you already have an audience via newsletter or social.
All take a cut per sale only; no fixed monthly cost.

## Launch checklist

1. Screenshot set (6): The Line roster with three offers, the Workbench station rail, the Risk Gauge catching a vague word in a draft offer, the Price station, the finished Spec Sheet, and the Claude Copilot "Productize my messy service description" action.
2. Record a 30–45s GIF/video: paste a messy service description into Copilot → build the offer station by station → watch the Risk Gauge go from red to green → print the Spec Sheet. The gauge flipping color is the money shot.
3. Landing copy: lead with the pain ("You already know your pricing has a hole in it — you just watched it happen last month"), then three bullets — a built-in scope-creep word detector, a conveyor-style offer builder, and Claude prompts that write your sales page from your own data.
4. Export the Kiln & Vance demo Spec Sheet as a real PDF and attach it as a free sample — the artifact sells the tool.
5. Post where "how do I price my services" pain lives: r/freelance, r/consulting, r/smallbusiness, Indie Hackers, freelance Slack/Discord communities, and productized-service Twitter/X.
6. Launch-week coupon (20%) with a hard end date — the app's own advice to buyers ("one price, no open-ended offers") applies to the coupon too.
7. Collect 3 testimonials from early buyers, specifically asking what vague phrase the Risk Gauge caught them using — it is the single most shareable detail about the tool.

## Upsell paths

- **Setup service ($79–$149):** sit with a buyer for one session, feed their three most-quoted services through the productizer, and ship all three offers together.
- **Template packs ($9–$19):** pre-filled offer sets per niche — web design sprints, bookkeeping cleanups, social media management, virtual-assistant retainers — each with realistic deliverables and boundaries already written.
- **Niche editions:** re-skin the vague-word library for a specific trade (legal, medical billing, construction bids) where the "risky phrase" list differs from general services; sell as a separate SKU at the same price.
- **Vague-word library refresh:** a quarterly add-on updating the scope-creep term list based on real buyer-submitted examples.
