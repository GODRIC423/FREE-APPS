# Meeting ROI Auditor — Monetization Notes

## Who buys it

- **Sales leaders and RevOps managers at 15–150 person B2B companies** who inherited a calendar full of recurring meetings and need a numbers-first case (not a vibe) to cut some of them without a political fight.
- **Founders and COOs of scaling startups** (Series A–C) watching headcount cost rise and suspecting — correctly — that a growing share of it is going to meetings that don't touch a deal.
- **Fractional COOs, ops consultants, and management consultants** who run "meeting audits" as a billable engagement and want a polished, client-shareable tool instead of a spreadsheet they rebuild every time.

The pain in all three cases is the same: everyone *feels* over-meetinged, but "we have too many meetings" doesn't survive a budget conversation. A loaded-cost number per meeting, and a reclaimed-hours-per-week counter that updates live, does.

## Positioning

1. "Every recurring meeting has a loaded cost, whether anyone's run the math or not. This tool runs the math."
2. "Cut meetings like an operator, not a martyr — Keep, Shrink, Kill, or make it async, with the dollar figure attached to every verdict."
3. "The time-and-motion study your calendar has never had. Stopwatch dial included."

## Suggested pricing

- **One-time purchase: $19–$39.** This sits comfortably in the "impulse buy for a real operational tool" range — priced like a premium spreadsheet template but delivered as a working app with real interactivity, live cost math, and AI-prompt integration.
- **Bundle tier: $79–$129** if packaged with 2–3 adjacent BD/ops tools from this same suite (e.g., paired with a pipeline or cadence tool) as an "Operator's Toolkit" — bundling consistently outperforms single-tool sales for utility apps like this.
- **Consultant/agency license: $149–$249** — a slightly higher tier explicitly permitting use across multiple client engagements (still single-purchasing-entity, not resale), aimed at the fractional-COO buyer who will reuse it repeatedly and bill hours around it.

## Where to sell — free-to-start marketplaces

- **Gumroad** — best fit for this kind of single-file utility tool; simple checkout, easy to bundle, good discovery within "productivity/business tools."
- **Lemon Squeezy** — handles tax/VAT automatically, good if selling internationally to consultants and agencies.
- **Payhip** — low-friction alternative with built-in license-key support if you want to gate against casual resharing.
- **Ko-fi Shop** — lower intent but zero-friction for an audience already following you for BD/sales-ops content.

## Launch checklist

1. **Screenshot set** — hero view with the stopwatch dial and demo data loaded, a meeting card mid-verdict (showing the reclaimed-hours callout), the Weekly Audit verdict chart, and the Meeting Policy Builder.
2. **10–20 second demo GIF** — record stamping a Kill verdict on a meeting and watching the header's reclaimed-hours number and stopwatch ring update live; that single interaction sells the tool better than any copy.
3. **Landing copy built around one number** — lead with a sample outcome from the demo scenario ("This 11-meeting demo audit finds ~$50k/year in reclaimed loaded cost") rather than a feature list.
4. **Post in operator-facing communities** — r/sales, r/Entrepreneur, RevOps Slack/Discord communities, Indie Hackers, and any "ops tools" newsletter roundups (many run weekly "tools I found" features and will take submissions).
5. **LinkedIn post from a founder/ops angle** — "I built a tool that puts a dollar sign on every recurring meeting" performs well as a native LinkedIn post with a screenshot, especially tagged toward RevOps/sales-ops audiences.
6. **Cross-list into the sibling BD-30 bundle page** if/when the full 30-app suite goes live, so this tool benefits from bundle-page traffic even as a standalone listing.
7. **Set up a simple "before/after" testimonial ask** — email the first 5–10 buyers a week after purchase asking what they killed and what it reclaimed; a real dollar figure in a testimonial converts far better than generic praise.

## Upsell paths

- **Setup/audit-as-a-service** — offer a paid 1-hour session where you (or a hired ops person) run a client's actual calendar through the tool live and hand them a finished policy doc. This tool is the deliverable engine for a $300–$800 service package.
- **Industry-specific policy packs** — pre-built Meeting Policy rule sets for agencies, SaaS sales orgs, or professional services firms, sold as a small add-on ($9–$19) that imports directly via the JSON import feature.
- **"Quarterly re-audit" subscription add-on** — not a SaaS subscription for the tool itself, but a light-touch service tier where you send buyers a reminder + fresh demo-data comparison each quarter to re-run their audit (keeps the tool relevant instead of a one-time download that gets forgotten).
- **Team/multi-seat license** — a higher price point bundling 5 activation "seats" (in practice, 5 separate copies of the HTML file with a shared invoice) for sales teams that want each manager auditing their own meetings independently.

## License note

Buyers receive the single self-contained `index.html` file (plus the editable `src/` source — JSX, CSS, and build config) under a **single-user / single-business license**: one person or one company may use, modify, and self-host the tool for their own internal purposes. Resale, redistribution as a competing product, or sublicensing to third parties (beyond the consultant-license tier's explicit multi-client-use permission) is not included. State clearly on the sales page that no ongoing support or updates are guaranteed unless sold as part of a service package — this is a tool sale, not a SaaS subscription.
