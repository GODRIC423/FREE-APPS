# Traction Bullseye — How to sell it

## Who buys it

Early-stage founders, indie hackers, and fractional marketers hunting for their first repeatable acquisition channel — people who've read *Traction* (or absorbed the Bullseye Framework secondhand) and know the theory but have nowhere to actually run it. The pain: without a forcing structure, they test five channels for a week each, none get a fair shot, and six months later there's still no answer to "where do our customers come from."

## Positioning (pick one)

1. "Nineteen channels, three rings, one rule: only three active at a time. Stop guessing your way to your first 100 customers."
2. "The Bullseye Framework, finally something you can click — cheap tests, honest results, a guardrail that keeps you from spreading thin."
3. "Every growth channel you've ever heard of, in one board, with the discipline to actually finish testing one before starting the next."

## Suggested pricing

- **One-time: $19–$35.** It replaces a spreadsheet, a whiteboard, and a re-read of the *Traction* chapter — single-file delivery justifies impulse pricing, and the embedded Copilot prompts (which turn Claude into a channel strategist for the price of a $20/mo subscription the buyer likely already has) are the premium hook.
- Optional tiers: $19 app only · $35 app + "19-Channel Cheap Test Playbook" PDF (one battle-tested cheap-test idea per channel) · $89 bundle with sibling BD apps (Lead Magnet Lab, Niche Validator, BD Command Deck) for a full go-to-market starter kit.

## Where to sell free-to-start

- **Gumroad** — fastest start, good for an indie-hacker or bootstrapper audience that already recognizes the *Traction* framework.
- **Lemon Squeezy** — cleaner checkout, handles EU VAT automatically.
- **Payhip** or **Ko-fi Shop** — zero monthly fee alternatives, good for a pay-what-you-want launch week.
- Screenshot the demo board everywhere — the concentric-ring hero is the ad; it reads instantly even to someone who has never heard of the framework.

## Launch checklist

1. Capture 4 screenshots: the full board with the demo loaded (21/100, 3/3 active), the channel panel with an experiment card open, the Learnings feed with real entries, and a generated Range Officer prompt.
2. Record a 30–45s GIF: load demo → click an Untested channel → attempt to promote it (guardrail warning fires) → demote a Focus channel → promote successfully.
3. Write the landing page around one line: "You don't have a traction problem. You have a focus problem. Three rings fix that."
4. Post the build story and GIF on Indie Hackers, r/startups, r/SaaS, and any *Traction*-adjacent communities (the book has a devoted following — search where people discuss it).
5. Offer 15–20 launch copies at half price in exchange for a testimonial; feature the two sharpest quotes on the page.
6. Publish one free artifact from the app — e.g., "The 19 Traction Channels, one-line each" as a lead magnet PDF or post — linking back to the paid tool.
7. Ship a v1.1 within two weeks based on buyer feedback (common ask will likely be more channel-specific test templates); email every buyer — file delivery makes updates a real selling point.

## Upsell paths

- **Setup service ($100–$250):** a 45-minute call where you help the buyer pick their first 3 channels, write real hypotheses, and load their first experiments onto the board.
- **Template packs:** a "cheap test starter" per channel (pre-filled hypothesis + test steps) sold as an add-on JSON import.
- **Niche editions:** re-skinned presets for specific business models — agencies, SaaS, physical-product brands — with different demo data and channel weighting notes.
- **Bundle:** cross-sell the other BizDev-30 apps at a bundle discount, especially Lead Magnet Lab and Niche Validator, which feed naturally into channel selection.
