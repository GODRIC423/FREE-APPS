# Proposal Studio — How to Sell It

## Who buys it

Freelancers, independent consultants, and 1–10 person agencies who close work with written proposals and lose deals to documents that look improvised. Their pain: every proposal starts from a stale Google Doc clone, scope language leaks money for months, and "design a nice PDF" costs an evening they bill at $150/hour. They already pay for Claude; they do not want another $29/month SaaS with their client data in it.

## Positioning (pick one)

1. "The proposal that feels like a decision already made — assembled in an hour, printed like fine stationery."
2. "Stop sending Google Docs to $30,000 clients. A private, offline proposal studio with a built-in scope-creep auditor."
3. "Your Claude subscription just became a proposal department: draft, tighten, red-team, and print — no API key, no data leaves your machine."

## Suggested pricing

- **One-time: $29–$49.** It replaces one evening of formatting on the very first proposal and touches revenue directly (pricing tables, scope defense), which supports the high end. Above $49 buyers expect e-signature and CRM sync, which this deliberately does not do.
- Optional tiers: **Personal $29** (single user) · **Studio $79** (up to 5 seats at one firm + priority email support).
- Bundle: sell with Case Study Factory and Battlecard Bay as a "Close the Deal" pack at $79–$99.

## Where to sell free-to-start

- **Gumroad** — fastest start, discover feed, pay-what-you-want option for launch week.
- **Lemon Squeezy** — cleaner checkout and EU VAT handled if selling into Europe.
- **Payhip** — no monthly fee, good coupon tooling for launch discounts.
- **Ko-fi Shop** — good if you already have a tip-jar audience.
All take a cut per sale only; no fixed monthly cost.

## Launch checklist

1. Screenshot set (6): cover sheet, Stationery Drawer, drag-ordering, Investment tab with two editions, the Foil Gauge dial, and the printed PDF side-by-side with the app.
2. Record a 30–45s GIF/video: Load demo → drag a section → star an edition → Print preview. The cream-paper preview is the money shot.
3. Landing copy: lead with the pain ("Your proposal is the last interview you'll ever get with this client"), then the 3 bullets — 11 starter sections, live-priced editions, Claude Copilot prompts.
4. Export the Harbor Lane demo as a real PDF and attach it as a free sample — the artifact sells the tool.
5. Post where proposal pain lives: r/freelance, r/consulting, Indie Hackers, LinkedIn (use the app to write the post), freelance Slack/Discord communities.
6. Launch-week coupon (20%) with a hard end date — mirrors the app's own "valid until" advice.
7. Collect 3 testimonials from early buyers in exchange for a niche edition vote (see below).

## Upsell paths

- **Setup service ($99–$199):** load their branding, write their canonical 11 sections and standard terms with them in one call.
- **Template packs ($9–$19):** pre-filled proposal sets per niche — web design, fractional CFO, marketing retainer, construction bid.
- **Niche editions:** re-skin + re-worded section library for legal, architecture, or agencies; sell as separate SKUs at the same price.
- **Prompt pack refresh:** quarterly update of the Copilot prompt library for buyers on a small annual add-on.

## License note

Buyer receives the single self-contained `index.html` plus full source (`src/`), for use on any of their own devices. Suggested license: single-user, unlimited proposals, no redistribution or resale of the app itself; team use via the Studio tier. All proposal content the buyer creates is theirs alone — the app stores nothing outside their browser.
