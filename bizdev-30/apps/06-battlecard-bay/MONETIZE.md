# Battlecard Bay — How to sell it

## Who buys it

Founders doing their own sales, first sales hires, and fractional/agency BD consultants at companies with 1–10 sellers. Their pain: they lose winnable deals to the same 2–4 competitors because competitive knowledge lives in one person's head, and enterprise competitive-intel tools (Klue, Crayon) start at five figures a year with mandatory demos. They want armed battlecards this week, not a platform rollout.

## Positioning (pick one)

1. "Competitor battlecards that win deals — the war room Klue charges $15k/yr for, in one file you own."
2. "Stop improvising against the same competitor. Arm a battlecard once, quick-draw the counter mid-call."
3. "Your competitive playbook + a $20 Claude subscription = a competitive-intel analyst on demand."

## Suggested pricing

- **One-time: $29–$59** for a single-user license. Rationale: it replaces a spreadsheet nobody maintains, not a SaaS; impulse-buy range for a tool that plausibly saves one deal. Anchor against Klue/Crayon annual pricing in the sales copy.
- Optional tiers: **$29 Solo** (the app) / **$79 Team Pack** (license for up to 5 reps + the Copilot prompt pack as a standalone PDF) / **$149 Consultant** (rebrand-and-deploy rights for client engagements — a natural fit for fractional sales leaders).

## Where to sell free-to-start

- **Gumroad** — fastest to launch, discoverability, pay-what-you-want option for launch week.
- **Lemon Squeezy** — cleaner checkout and EU VAT handled if selling to European sales consultants.
- **Payhip** or **Ko-fi** — zero monthly fee alternatives; Ko-fi works well if you build an audience with competitive-selling content.
- Bundle listing on **AppSumo Marketplace** later, once reviews exist.

## Launch checklist

1. Load the demo fleet and capture 4 screenshots: radar fleet view, an armed battlecard, Action Stations mid-search, and the Copilot panel with a generated prompt.
2. Record a 30–45s GIF/video: prospect objection typed into Action Stations → counter appears in big type → proof point read out.
3. Write the landing page around one story: "the deal you lost last quarter to the competitor you knew was beatable" — then the three pitches above as sections.
4. Publish on Gumroad with launch pricing ($19 first week), license note, and the GIF above the fold.
5. Post the build story on LinkedIn (where the buyers are): "I turned my competitive notes into a war room" with the radar screenshot; repost a counter-writing tip weekly.
6. Ship a free companion: "10 counter-battery rows against 'the incumbent'" as a PDF lead magnet that links to the paid app.
7. Answer competitive-selling questions in r/sales and founder Slack/Discord communities with genuinely useful counters; link the tool in your profile, not the posts.

## Upsell paths

- **Setup service ($150–$400):** a 90-minute call where you build the buyer's first three battlecards with them, using the Copilot recon prompts live.
- **Battlecard template packs ($15–$29):** pre-armed cards per market — "vs. the enterprise incumbent", "vs. the cheap point tool", "vs. doing nothing" — importable JSON.
- **Niche editions:** re-skinned builds for agencies, MSPs, recruiters ("Bake-off Bay"), each with domain demo data.
- **Quarterly wargame retainer:** for consultants — run the roleplay + fleet-brief ritual with a client's team every quarter.
