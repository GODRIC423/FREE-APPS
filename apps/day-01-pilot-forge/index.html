      Operator’s card — how to work the table

      Pilot Forge is a drafting table for AI pilots. A rough “I could build something for that business” goes on the sheet; a scoped, priced, provable pilot comes off it — schematic, fit gauge, loss calculation, proof schedule, work orders, and an issued brief.

      1. Pin up the sample job (header button) to see a finished drawing — Rivera Heating & Air, missed-call rescue — or start on a clean sheet.
      2. Zone A — Specification. Pick the business type, the wedge (the one leak this pilot fixes), and write the owner’s pain in one concrete sentence with a number in it. The schematic at the top redraws as you type.
      3. Define the proof the owner will hold — a report, log, or tracker — and set the gate. Draft-only reads best on the gauge: it is the safest thing to sell first.
      4. Read the fit gauge. The needle sweeps 0–100; the inspection record shows every factor’s points and a fix when it runs weak. Work the lowest line until the needle clears 60.
      5. Zone B — Loss calculation. Missed leads, close rate, job value, recovery rate. The leak, recoverable revenue, suggested fee, and payback rule themselves out live.
      6. Zone C — Schedule of proof. Milestones are drafted from your wedge and proof window. Tick them, redraw days and wording, add your own, or redraft the whole schedule.
      7. Zone D — Work orders. Cut cards, then drag them (or walk them with the arrows) across Queued / On the bench / Signed off. Striking a card offers Undo.
      8. Zone E — Issue. Copy the Markdown brief, file a JSON copy, reload it later, or print the issued sheet. The stamp reads APPROVED FOR PROOF once the gauge clears 60 — until then the drawing holds.
