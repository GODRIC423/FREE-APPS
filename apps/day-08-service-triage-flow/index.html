        How to use Service Triage Flow

        Guide a call-taker through triaging an inbound service request: capture the call, spot urgency cues, walk the safety checklist, and hand off a routing recommendation — without promising anything.

        1. Load the demo. A realistic restaurant AC call lands on the review step so you can see urgency, category, route, and reasons immediately.
        2. Step 1 · Caller. Capture who is calling, a callback number, the site, and the service type. Caller and site are required.
        3. Step 2 · Issue. Type what the caller says in their own words. Urgency cue chips light up as you type — negated phrases like “no smoke” are ignored. Flag vulnerable occupants and business impact.
        4. Step 3 · Safety. Walk the hazard checklist out loud. A severe hazard (gas, smoke, CO, live wiring) shows a caller-safety script and offers a fast-track straight to review.
        5. Step 4 · Logistics. Access notes, on-site window, constraints, repeat/warranty context, and whether the caller wants an estimate.
        6. Step 5 · Review & route. Check the urgency score, category, route, and the reasons behind them. Override the category if your judgment differs — the auto suggestion stays on record. Confirm the no-promises boundary, then Log this call.
        7. Call log. Each logged call keeps its snapshot. Print a triage card, copy its Markdown, or delete it — Undo is available for 7 seconds.
        8. Export & handoff. Copy Markdown, print the card, download or import a JSON backup, or export the log as CSV. Every output is a draft for human review.
